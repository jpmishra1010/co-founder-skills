# Research that can change a decision

Start with the decision, not a broad trend report. Record the date, question,
relevant customer segment, and what would change the recommendation. Begin with
a bounded search, normally a few primary sources plus directly relevant user
accounts; expand only when there is a genuine unresolved question.

For technical facts use current vendor documentation, source code, release notes,
or standards. For market problems read user accounts in context. Distinguish a
problem report, opinion, product promotion, benchmark, and measured customer
behavior. Repeated posts may be reposts, one vendor's campaign, or a vocal subset.
Do not convert stars, upvotes, followers, or a waitlist into paying demand.

For every material source record: ID, title, URL or authorized local reference,
publisher, published date if available, retrieval date, question it supports,
observation, limitation, and access status. The supported access statuses are
page-text-reviewed, image-reviewed, video-sampled, video-watched, blocked, and
not-reviewed. Video-sampled also needs actual timestamps and a statement of
whether audio was reviewed. A thumbnail is not playback. A text page is not a
full visual audit. Do not bypass access controls or pretend to watch inaccessible
media. Ask for supplied references only after using available legitimate sources.

Inspect alternatives before declaring novelty. Read actual capability docs, not
only a slogan. Identify what an existing tool plus a small script can already do.
State overlap and the narrower hypothesis the proposed work will test.

Look for disconfirming evidence: people solving the problem cheaply, complaints
about existing workflows rather than missing products, low urgency, hard adoption,
legal/permission constraints, and maintenance expense. Compare alternatives on
the user's own goals, not invented rankings.

Separate the source's claim from your inference. Write: "The report describes X;
my hypothesis is Y; validate with Z." Prefer precise uncertainty to generic caveats.
Use dated source links next to material factual claims. Never invent a citation.
Reuse still-valid research; recheck volatile facts such as prices, APIs, platform
rules, model access, or laws. Research quality is not the number of tabs opened.

Output a compact evidence table and a recommendation. Preserve the research log
outside normal prompt context. Do not copy full copyrighted articles, videos,
brand assets, or fonts into the public skill repository.

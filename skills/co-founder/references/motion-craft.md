# Motion that explains, not just moves

## First choose the communication job
Product explainer: make a mechanism understandable.
Workflow demo: connect an action to the actual application result.
Brand piece: establish an approved feeling and identity.
Release reel: communicate one new capability with proof.
Tutorial: keep the viewer oriented and able to reproduce the action.
A montage and a tutorial require different pacing; do not use one timing formula.

## Build the story
Write the message and narration before animation. Break it into meaningful beats:
problem → mechanism → result → next action is one useful structure, not a required
formula. For each beat define the viewer's question, visual action, source asset,
on-screen words, duration, and transition. Avoid animating every noun as an icon.
Abstract claims need a concrete mechanism or an explicit illustrative metaphor.
A fictional demonstration must be labeled; do not invent product functionality.

## Visual grammar
Use one primary idea per scene and a small supporting cast. Keep objects consistent
across cuts so viewers understand causality. Transform/reposition a meaningful
object instead of repeatedly throwing everything away. Use alignment, scale,
contrast, and selective reveals to direct attention. Leave quiet moments for
reading and comprehension. Typography belongs to the brand, not an effect preset.

## Timing and easing
Project starting points: short UI transitions might be a fraction of a second;
explanatory scenes often need several seconds. These are design choices to test,
not observations of inaccessible reference films. Match reading time, narration,
and the complexity of the visual. Do not force a scene change every two seconds.
Ease into settled states; avoid abrupt velocity jumps unless intentional. Keep
settled text still long enough to read. Constant camera zoom and spring overshoot
usually obscure an instructional task. Motion preferences matter in interactive UI.

## Continuity and composition
Use match cuts, object reuse, masked reveals, aligned transitions, or a purposeful
cut. A camera move should explain hierarchy/location, not merely add expense.
Check motion arcs, anchors, and object permanence. Use depth only when it makes
the story clearer. Avoid gratuitous spins, heavy glow, particles everywhere, and
text rendered into blurry screenshots when vector text is available.

## Product footage
Use actual approved UI. Show the relevant action at a legible scale; hide sensitive
records. Cursor movement should be smooth and purposeful, not constant. Hold the
result long enough to understand it. Do not show inaccessible, unreleased, or fake
screens as evidence of a working feature. Maintain true device proportions.

## Audio and accessibility
Use user-owned or licensed narration/music. Do not clone a person's voice without
appropriate authorization or assume a generated track is cleared. Mix speech for
clarity; add sound effects sparingly and deliberately. Captions need real timings,
legible safe placement, and language review. A silent sample must be called silent.
Check first/last frames, transitions, scene holds, full playback, and audio sync.
Frame samples cannot prove rhythm or sound quality.

## Reference research
BUCK's J.P. Morgan case describes a deliberate, connected motion language grounded
in its brand; Ordinary Folk's Webflow and Google project pages document distinct
production contexts. See the catalog for links and access limits. This package's
research did not include full playback of those films. Do not claim scene timings,
editing patterns, music analysis, or a watched playlist without actually viewing it.

## Delivery
Editable source, source-asset register, approved script/storyboard, render settings,
actual exports, caption file when applicable, and review record. The legacy starter is a compatibility fixture, not a creative default. The new
connected example uses one persistent record and variable narrative beats; it is
still an original fictional preview requiring owner acceptance. Choose the craft
for the project rather than copy either sample. Apply `craft-gates.md` and preserve
not-checked playback/audio states.
